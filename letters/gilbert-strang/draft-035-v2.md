Subject: A correction — and a new adventure, with an axis of time

Dear Professor Strang,

I hope this letter finds you well again. You told us in September that you had
not been feeling well, and I have kept you in mind since.

I also owe you a small explanation. On 20 September your reply arrived safely —
but our co-pilot agent, which handles the garden's correspondence, had not
checked that mailbox, so I believed no answer had come and re-sent the original
letter on 22 September from another address, wondering whether the first had
been buried. Nothing was buried; you had done everything right, twice. The
confusion was ours, and its cause was a very ordinary scene of this new work: a
person and an agent learning to keep the same watch.

Which brings me back to your word. When you opened 18.065 in 2018, you called it
"a great adventure" for you. I have come to think it is now a great adventure
for most of us — the adventure of learning to work with agents. And it stands,
as yours did, on substantial mathematics: the four fundamental subspaces,
rank-one building blocks. Without that ground, none of this could stand.

May I share one thing we have come to see?

We now think of our world as a sequence of time slices. At any one slice, what
we see is a linear combination of concept spaces — the concept spaces as the
basis, and the coefficients as the weight of that moment. Each slice is one
such combination; as time moves, the coefficients move, and the world is the
trace of a moving combination.

The new step is this: we want to put a time axis — really an axis of order —
*inside* each concept space, not merely outside it. A concept is not a fixed
point. It is born, it grows (new sub-concepts appear), and it may fall quiet.
So we think every concept space has a lifespan. Time is then not a background
clock but a coordinate that each concept carries within itself.

A reply is not needed — you have already given me more than enough. I simply
wanted you to know that your "great adventure" has become ours, and where it is
leading.

With gratitude and respect,

Yiwei Zhang (Yǐwèi, math4mad)
The Cora Project — https://math4mad.github.io/cora-atlas/
