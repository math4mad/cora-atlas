## English body（正文，净本待抽 send-037-v0.txt）

Dear Professor Yamanaka,

I write not as a biologist but as someone who has spent years on a narrow question — how a *state* relates to *time* — and who found, in your 2006 work, the cleanest empirical case for a point that is otherwise hard to argue.

When Takahashi & Yamanaka (2006) showed that four factors could bring a somatic cell toward a pluripotent state, the natural word for it was "reversal." May I offer, with great humility, one small shift in the picture: **the iPS colony is not a return to the starting point; it is a branch that arises at a later time point and resembles the start.** Daley & Hochedlinger (2010) make this concrete — the epigenetic memory that lingers (a blood-derived iPS more readily returns to blood) is precisely what a *branch* carries and a true *return* could not: the residue of where it came from.

I attach a single figure: differentiation runs forward from the zygote (T₀); the iPS colony is a branch that arises at Tₖ, sits at the pluripotency level, and is nonetheless downstream — *like, but not the same as* T₀.

I claim no correction to your science; the biology is yours. I only note that your result is, for me, the strongest evidence for the **irreversibility of time**: even an artificially forced "reversal" does not reverse cleanly.

If the figure is of any interest, it is yours to use or to discard. Thank you for the work.

With admiration and respect,
math4mad
