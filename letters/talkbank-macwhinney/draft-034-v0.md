Dear Professor MacWhinney, dear Dr. Zhang,

Brian — thank you for a reply that arrived faster than our download attempts
ever succeeded. Three things you confirmed we will carry carefully:
developmental profiles built on large corpora is the right direction, derived
measures may be published, and the Chinese holdings of CHILDES are larger than
we had dared to hope. And thank you for making the introduction explicit —
that is the most useful thing a corpus steward can do.

Yibin — let me introduce the garden briefly. We are a small independent
research group in China (led by a former statistics teacher, no institution,
no commercial interest). Our object of study is the concept space as a
measurable geometry: concepts are regions, categorization is a
maximum-similarity anchoring decision, and learning is sequential Bayesian
updating that never resets — today's posterior is tomorrow's prior. Read
through this machinery, the anchor mechanism turns out to be exactly what
developmental psychology calls centration: the engine is, by construction, a
child's mind — so we decided to give it a child's education.

The experiment is called the AGE-LADDER: an age-tiered curriculum (board-book
catalogs and public-domain readers for 0–5; CHILDES-style child-directed
speech as the dialogue layer; historical graded readers like McGuffey for
6–15; adult web text only as the "terminal snapshot" control). The central
test is deliberately minimal: the same bag of data, the same seeds, two
feeding orders — stage-ordered versus shuffled — and the readout is not
benchmark scores but the trajectory of internal parameters: interference
waves in held-out cross-entropy, Ebbinghaus-style savings on re-exposure, and
the migration of concept regions in weight space. On a 0.5B model the
order-effect has already passed pre-registered criteria 3/3 on a toddler bag,
and 2/3 on an adult seven-domain bag with a dose-response in schedule
granularity. We are not competing with scale; we are measuring what order
leaves behind.

This is where we need a friend on the ground, and Brian says you are the one.
Three concrete asks:

1. **The Chinese layer.** Which Mandarin/Cantonese collections in CHILDES
   carry usable age labels, and which are longitudinal? A Chinese dialogue
   layer would let the ladder be built in the language our engine actually
   thinks in — and your own corpora, if any are open to us, would be equally
   welcome. All use follows TalkBank rules: per-corpus citation,
   acknowledgment of NICHD support, derived measures only, no re-hosting of
   raw CHAT files, and absolutely no commercial use.
2. **A mainland-side path.** Our connection can browse TalkBank but bulk
   operations (the getCHAT endpoint, @Explore downloads) reset persistently
   from here. Is there a mirror, a batch-transfer arrangement, or an ECNU-side
   route through which a registered user could legitimately obtain the
   collections we cite? We are patient and small — we just need one door that
   stays open.
3. **A reciprocal offer.** Everything we build on this — the ladder metrics
   (interference-wave asymmetry, savings amplitude, order-dose curves), the
   pre-registration templates, the analysis code — will be released for use
   on child corpora, including yours. A measurement instrument is only as
   good as the data it has been tested on, and Chinese child-directed speech
   is the largest gap in that testing today.

If any of this is worth a conversation, I am happy to send a one-page
summary of the protocol, or to travel to Shanghai when bandwidth, like
friendship, needs a physical layer.
